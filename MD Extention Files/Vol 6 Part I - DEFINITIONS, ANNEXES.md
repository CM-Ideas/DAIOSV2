# **CHAPTER 325**

# **CONTROLLED TERMINOLOGY CONSTITUTION**

## **325.1 Executive Principle**

One concept shall have one governed meaning.

The same term shall not carry conflicting meanings across:

- volumes;

- policies;

- prompts;

- systems;

- dashboards;

- reports;

- teams.

## **325.2 Controlled Term**

A Controlled Term is a word, phrase, acronym, label, status, category or code whose official meaning is governed through Volume 6.

## **325.3 Controlled Terminology Purposes**

Controlled terminology shall:

- eliminate ambiguity;

- reduce duplication;

- improve system integration;

- standardize AI outputs;

- support search;

- improve reporting;

- preserve institutional meaning;

- prevent unauthorized reinterpretation.

## **325.4 Controlled Terminology Scope**

Controlled terminology includes:

- constitutional terms;

- product terms;

- architecture terms;

- AI terms;

- security terms;

- revenue terms;

- KPI terms;

- student terms;

- faculty terms;

- startup terms;

- alumni terms;

- status values;

- risk values;

- decision values;

- approval values;

- lifecycle values.

## **325.5 No Informal Replacement**

An approved Controlled Term shall not be replaced by a different term in a formal system or document without:

- equivalence review;

- impact analysis;

- approval;

- version update;

- migration plan where necessary.

# **CHAPTER 326**

# **DEFINITION AUTHORITY AND HIERARCHY**

## **326.1 Definition Hierarchy**

Where definitions conflict, the following order shall apply:

- Volume 0 constitutional definitions;

- definition explicitly contained in Volumes 1–5;

- Volume 6 Master Definition Register;

- approved policy definition;

- approved SOP definition;

- product-specific definition;

- contextual or operational usage.

## **326.2 Explicit Definition**

A definition explicitly stated in the governing constitutional chapter takes priority over a general glossary definition.

## **326.3 Product-Specific Definition**

A product may define a specialized term only when:

- the term is not constitutionally controlled;

- the definition does not conflict;

- the specialized scope is stated;

- the term is registered.

## **326.4 Undefined Term**

Where a material term is not defined:

- identify the intended meaning;

- check existing registers;

- request interpretation where needed;

- create a proposed controlled definition;

- obtain approval before broad use.

## **326.5 Interpretation Authority**

Material ambiguity shall be referred to the designated Constitutional Interpretation Authority under P00.0.

Forms and templates shall operationalize constitutional requirements.

- Intake Form;

- Declaration Form;

- Approval Form;

- Review Form;

- Assessment Form;

- Amendment Form;

- Interpretation Form;

- Exception Form;

- Risk Form;

- Decision Form;

- Closure Form;

- Freeze Form.

## **351.2 Template Requirements**

Every controlled form or template must include:

- Form ID;

- title;

- purpose;

- owner;

- version;

- status;

- instructions;

- required fields;

- approval;

- related clauses.

## **351.3 Mandatory Field**

A mandatory field shall not be removed without controlled change.

## **351.4 Digital Form**

Digital forms should support:

- validation;

- identity;

- time stamp;

- attachment;

- workflow;

- approval;

- audit;

- register update.

# **CHAPTER 353**

# **PART I INTERIM DECLARATION**

A constitution becomes unreliable when:

- terms have different meanings;

- documents use random names;

- IDs are duplicated;

- registers conflict;

- versions are overwritten;

- annexes are uncontrolled;

- forms omit mandatory evidence;

- cross-references break;

- teams create shadow lists;

- systems interpret statuses differently.

DAIOS shall therefore ensure:

- One Term, One Governed Meaning.

- One Entity, One Stable Identifier.

- One Document, One Controlled Status.

- One Register, One Authoritative Source.

- One KPI, One Definition.

- One Prompt, One Registered Version.

- One Amendment, One Traceable Record.

- One Anchor, One Approved Purpose.

- One Constitution, Many Connected Implementations.

The governing constitutional administration cycle is:

Define
→ Code
→ Register
→ Reference
→ Implement
→ Review
→ Amend
→ Version
→ Archive
→ Preserve Memory.
