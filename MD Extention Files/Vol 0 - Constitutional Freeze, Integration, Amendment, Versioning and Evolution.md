## **Constitution Inclusion Test**

A change belongs in the Constitution only when it defines or alters:

- enduring principles;

- mandatory enterprise obligations;

- authority or decision rights;

- governance gates;

- rights, duties, or accountability;

- mandatory evidence;

- enterprise-wide prohibitions;

- constitutional architecture rules;

- mandatory security or AI governance;

- commercialization authority;

- amendment or exception procedure.

Temporary, technical, product-specific, vendor-specific, or operational details should normally be placed in prompts, registries, SOPs, standards, or configuration—not in the Constitution.

- clarification;

- cross-reference correction;

- non-substantive editorial correction.

# **23. Freeze Decision Rules**

Issue **Freeze Ready** only when:

- no critical unresolved conflict exists;

- approval authority is clear;

- constitutional memory is operational;

- version metadata is complete;

- future anchors are defined;

- backward compatibility has been assessed;

- legal and regulatory obligations have been addressed;

- required registries and governance controls are available;

- all mandatory approvals have been received.

- no critical issue remains;

- limited non-critical actions remain;

Issue **Do Not Freeze** when:

- a critical conflict remains;

- authority is ambiguous;

- material legal or regulatory exposure is unresolved;

- architecture integrity is uncertain;

- AI governance is incomplete;

- amendment and memory controls are not ready;
