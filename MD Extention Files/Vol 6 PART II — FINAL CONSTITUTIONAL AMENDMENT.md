# **CHAPTER 354**

# **CONSTITUTIONAL CHANGE GOVERNANCE**

The DAIOS Constitution shall remain stable without becoming rigid.

Innovation may continue through:

- products;

- prompts;

- agents;

- models;

- workflows;

- SOPs;

- policies;

- registers;

- standards;

- annexes;

- controlled configurations.

The Constitution shall be amended only when a material constitutional requirement must change.

## **354.1 Constitutional Change Principle**

The governing principle is:

Freeze the Constitution without freezing innovation.

## **354.2 Change Routing**

Every proposed change must first determine whether it belongs in:

- the Constitution;

- a Constitutional Interpretation;

- a Normative Annex;

- a Policy;

- a Standard;

- an SOP;

- a Register;

- a Prompt;

- an Architecture Decision;

- a Configuration;

- a Temporary Exception.

## **354.3 No Automatic Amendment**

A new idea, technology, tool, product or business requirement shall not automatically justify constitutional amendment.

## **354.4 Amendment Necessity Test**

A constitutional amendment may be justified when the proposed change:

- alters constitutional authority;

- changes a fundamental principle;

- creates or removes binding obligations;

- changes decision rights;

- creates a new constitutional domain;

- resolves a material constitutional conflict;

- affects multiple volumes;

- changes protected terminology;

- changes freeze or amendment procedures;

- materially affects rights, safety, accountability or governance.

## **354.5 Operational Change**

Operational changes should normally remain outside the Constitution when they involve:

- product features;

- technology versions;

- vendor selection;

- model selection;

- pricing values;

- dashboard design;

- organizational assignment below constitutional authority;

- implementation schedule;

- workflow configuration;

- report layout.

# **CHAPTER 355**

# **CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENT CLASSIFICATIONS**

## **355.1 Administrative Correction**

A non-substantive correction addressing:

- spelling;

- numbering;

- formatting;

- broken reference;

- minor clerical error.

An Administrative Correction shall not change meaning, authority or obligation.

## **355.2 Clarifying Amendment**

A change that:

- removes ambiguity;

- improves wording;

- explains an existing obligation;

- preserves original intent.

## **355.3 Minor Amendment**

A controlled addition or revision that:

- adds limited scope;

- creates a non-disruptive obligation;

- updates terminology;

- adds an annex or registry requirement;

- does not alter constitutional architecture materially.

## **355.4 Major Amendment**

A material change that:

- changes constitutional scope;

- alters authority;

- creates a new governance body;

- changes rights or obligations;

- affects several volumes;

- requires major system or process migration.

## **355.5 Emergency Amendment**

A time-sensitive temporary or permanent amendment required to respond to:

- law;

- regulation;

- severe security threat;

- safety risk;

- major institutional risk;

- critical operational continuity;

- urgent strategic necessity.

## **355.6 Consolidating Amendment**

An amendment that integrates several approved amendments into a new consolidated constitutional version.

## **355.7 Repealing Amendment**

An amendment that formally removes or replaces an existing clause, chapter, annex or constitutional mechanism.

# **CHAPTER 356**

# **CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENT AUTHORITY**

## **356.1 Proposal Authority**

An amendment may be proposed by:

- Chairman;

- Board or constitutionally authorized governing body;

- Constitutional Governance Council;

- Executive Council;

- Architecture Review Board;

- EADC authority;

- DUDOS authority;

- authorized Product, Revenue, Intelligence or Human Ecosystem Council;

- P00.0 following constitutional analysis.

## **356.2 Approval Authority**

Approval authority depends on the amendment classification.

### **Administrative Correction**

May be approved by the Constitutional Secretary or delegated authority, subject to audit.

### **Clarifying or Minor Amendment**

Requires review by the Constitutional Governance Council and approval under the applicable D3 or D4 authority.

### **Major Amendment**

Requires strategic D4 approval and any additional governing-body approval required by institutional authority.

### **Emergency Amendment**

Requires the highest available authorized emergency authority and subsequent ratification.

## **356.3 Conflict of Interest**

A proposer or reviewer with a material conflict must disclose it and may be excluded from final approval.

## **356.4 No Self-Approval**

No individual shall independently propose, assess and finally approve a material amendment without required review.

# **CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENT LIFECYCLE**

## **357.1 Amendment Registration**

Every proposal must receive an Amendment ID before substantive review.

## **357.2 Duplicate Review**

P00.0 shall determine whether the proposed change:

- already exists;

- is already governed elsewhere;

- duplicates an anchor;

- belongs in a register or SOP;

- conflicts with prior amendments.

## **357.3 Amendment Owner**

Every amendment must have an accountable Amendment Owner.

## **357.4 Implementation Owner**

An approved amendment must identify who will update:

- systems;

- prompts;

- policies;

- SOPs;

- forms;

- registers;

- training;

- documentation.

## **357.5 Closure**

An amendment is not closed until implementation evidence is verified.

# **CHAPTER 364**

# **CONSTITUTIONAL INTERPRETATION GOVERNANCE**

A Constitutional Interpretation explains an existing requirement without changing its fundamental meaning.

## **364.1 Interpretation Purpose**

Interpretation may be requested when:

- wording is ambiguous;

- clauses appear inconsistent;

- new circumstances require clarification;

- implementation teams disagree;

- system behavior requires constitutional meaning.

## **364.2 Interpretation Restriction**

An interpretation shall not create a material new obligation that requires amendment.

## **364.3 Interpretation Authority**

Interpretations shall be approved by the designated Constitutional Interpretation Authority.

## **364.4 Interpretation Effect**

An approved interpretation becomes an authoritative guide until:

- superseded;

- incorporated into an amendment;

- retired;

- rendered unnecessary.

# **CHAPTER 366**

An exception permits a time-bound deviation from a constitutional or controlled requirement without permanently changing the requirement.

## **366.1 Exception Principle**

Exceptions shall be:

- rare;

- justified;

- time-bound;

- risk-assessed;

- approved;

- monitored;

- auditable.

## **366.2 Exception Types**

- Temporary Operational Exception;

- Architecture Exception;

- Security Exception;

- Commercial Exception;

- Data Exception;

- AI Exception;

- Academic Exception;

- Emergency Exception;

- Transitional Exception.

## **366.3 Prohibited Exception**

No exception may authorize:

- unlawful conduct;

- deliberate fraud;

- concealment;

- removal of non-waivable rights;

- uncontrolled critical safety risk;

- unauthorized constitutional amendment.

## **366.4 Compensating Control**

Every material exception must define controls reducing the additional risk.
