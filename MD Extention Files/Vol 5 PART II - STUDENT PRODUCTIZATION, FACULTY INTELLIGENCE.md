# **CHAPTER 252**

# **CAPSTONE PRODUCT FACTORY**

Capstone projects shall not automatically end as academic submissions.

Every eligible capstone shall be assessed for its potential to become:

- a reusable product;

- a commercial solution;

- a research output;

- an intellectual property asset;

- an industry solution;

- a startup;

- a marketplace product;

- an institutional service;

- an open-source contribution where appropriate.

## **252.1 Capstone Lifecycle**

Problem Identification
→ Research
→ Solution Design
→ Prototype
→ Academic Evaluation
→ Product Assessment
→ Technical Review
→ Market Validation
→ Commercialization Decision
→ Product, Startup, License, Research or Archive.

## **252.2 Capstone Registration**

Every eligible capstone must have:

- Capstone ID;

- title;

- programme;

- department;

- students;

- faculty supervisor;

- problem statement;

- intended users;

- technology;

- AI use;

- data;

- IP position;

- product potential;

- commercialization status;

- repository link;

- evaluation status.

## **252.3 Product Potential Assessment**

The assessment shall consider:

- problem significance;

- user demand;

- solution quality;

- technical feasibility;

- architecture;

- security;

- differentiation;

- scalability;

- reuse;

- integration;

- commercialization potential;

- social impact;

- global relevance.

## **252.4 Capstone Verdicts**

Permitted verdicts include:

- Academic Completion Only;

- Improve and Reassess;

- Institutional Pilot;

- Industry Pilot;

- Marketplace Candidate;

- Product Factory Candidate;

- Research Candidate;

- IP Protection Candidate;

- Startup Candidate;

- Open-Source Candidate;

- Archive with Knowledge Preservation.

## **252.5 No Automatic Commercialization**

Academic quality does not automatically establish market readiness.

Commercial progression requires evidence and approved gates.
